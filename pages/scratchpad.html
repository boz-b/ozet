Orientation

Progress has several meanings.

VHF began with a straightforward ambition: turn investment research into a disciplined process for selecting securities, controlling risk, simulating trades and evaluating results. The hard part has proved to be connecting those steps without losing the economic meaning of the inputs. A plausible ranking is only the beginning. The system must also know what it owned, when it could trade, what it paid, and what happened when a security stopped trading.

The first page explains that investment logic. This page follows the development decisions that made it concrete. It separates three questions throughout: Does the software implement its rules? Can the available historical evidence support the calculation? Does the calculation establish an investment advantage? A successful answer to the first does not settle the other two.

Implemented

A rule exists in code

Invented examples and replay can show that the rule behaves as intended. They cannot show that a strategy earns money in real markets.

Historically calculated

Real inputs were used

A result may still depend on modeled costs, incomplete prices or current data revisions. Those qualifications travel with the result.

Investment acceptance

The evidence is sufficient

This requires the relevant complete paths, justified comparison and testing, and a separate review. VHF has not reached live investment acceptance.

The record runs through 13 October 2026. Chapters 1–10 cover the earlier work, ending on 18 September. Chapters 11–15 continue from there through the October calculations and their results; chapter 16 proposes how to finish. A dated success means that particular task passed, not that the whole strategy was validated. “Long-only” means buying and owning shares, without selling borrowed shares. Older records use “signed” for portfolios that buy stocks and also sell borrowed stocks short. Read the plain-language terms.

01 · May 2026

Start with a common investment language.

The project was scaffolded on 5 May. Its first substantive source, Giuseppe A. Paleologo’s Advanced Portfolio Management, was incorporated on 12 May. It supplied an organizing idea: security selection, position sizing, diversification and risk control belong to one portfolio process. A collection of attractive trades can still make an unattractive portfolio if all the trades depend on the same risk.

The library then broadened. Colin Bennett’s volatility text introduced variance, correlation, hedging and the economics of derivatives. Wesley Gray and Jack Vogel’s Quantitative Momentum supplied a concrete equity-selection process. Donadio and Ghosh’s Learn Algorithmic Trading connected research to data handling, events, execution and risk checks. Paleologo’s The Elements of Quantitative Investing deepened the treatment of factor models, estimation, sizing and attribution.

Sources became linked explanations of concepts, strategies and relationships. The original documents remained separate from these interpretations. On 1 September, the Quantopian lecture collection strengthened existing material on regression, factor analysis, multiple testing, implementation shortfall and conditional risk.

An architectural correction in August made the separation sharper: the research library records what sources say; engineering records what VHF has built and measured. A software failure does not disprove a book’s investment argument. A passing test does not validate it. This distinction prevents development status from quietly rewriting the research.

02 · 11–15 August

Build a machine whose arithmetic can be checked.

The first isolated trading prototype appeared on 11 August. It connected momentum, a factor-risk model, portfolio construction, risk review, next-opening execution and profit-and-loss accounting. It used invented prices so that the expected behavior could be inspected without confusing implementation work with historical evidence.

Phase 1 hardened that connected example. It fixed identifiers, timing, numerical checks and accounting invariants, and added a beginner walkthrough. A fixed 650-session example became a compatibility reference. Its purpose was to detect unintended changes: rerunning the same declared example should reproduce the same economics. Its simulated return was never a market result.

Phase 2 made the process reconstructible. Every run declared its rules and produced a manifest, an event journal and derived outputs. Replay rebuilt holdings, cash, costs, drawdown and exposures from saved events. This matters because reproducing a final number is weaker evidence than showing how every change in the account produced it.

Phases 3–5 addressed three different kinds of discipline. The data contract separated a security’s permanent identity from its changing ticker and distinguished when an event occurred from when information became available. The operations layer modeled orders, share lots, price ticks, corporate actions, financing and trading halts. The risk layer added dated exposures and alternative covariance estimates, then constrained how a forecast could become a position.

The constrained optimizer also had a specific scope: it searched a declared path from a feasible starting portfolio. It was not a universal repair mechanism for every drifted or infeasible portfolio. Later development had to address that operational case directly.

03 · 18–23 August

Define an experiment before reading its answer.

Phase 6 made momentum the first substantial historical research target. This choice provided a manageable chain from investment idea to implementation: identify past winners, distinguish their return paths, form a portfolio, hold it according to a schedule and compare it with appropriate alternatives.

The primary design uses calendar-month momentum with the recent month skipped, followed by two quality measures: information discreteness, or FIP, describes how gains are distributed across up and down days; signed MAX measures the largest signed daily return in the skipped month. The underlying research is Frog in the Pan by Da, Gurun and Warachka and Maxing out by Bali, Cakici and Whitelaw. Within the high-momentum shortlist, the declared quality rule favors the specified lower-FIP and lower-MAX ranks. Fifty selected stocks receive 2% each. These ideas are anchored in Gray and Vogel’s momentum framework; exact data substitutions, tie rules and implementation amendments are VHF choices.

A separate daily 252/21 challenger combines momentum, the fit of a log-price trend and a penalty for the largest absolute daily move. Its formula is a VHF engineering choice. It should not be described as the same strategy with another label. The registry also includes factor-neutral and alternative holding schedules. Recording these choices in advance makes later experimentation visible instead of letting every changed parameter appear to be the original strategy.

04 · 23–27 August

Real data changed the questions.

VHF began evaluating Sharadar data in August, first through schema checks and then through preserved local source captures. A five-year bundle supported the first historical pilot. It provided a practical way to exercise security mapping, prices, market capitalization and corporate-action records on real observations.

These archives are current-vintage history: historical dates as represented in the acquired dataset. A one-session delay between formation and execution does not prove that every revision, listing classification or input was available to a researcher at the original historical time. VHF therefore kept this descriptive research view separate from its stricter point-in-time data contract.

The five-year pilot developed through several versions. It added terminal-event investigation, benchmark comparisons, turnover and illustrative cost/capacity sensitivities, followed by factor-target feasibility and route-coverage checks. Each step revealed why a ranking and two endpoint prices were insufficient. A stock could be present at selection and absent at the planned sale. A neutral target could be mathematically feasible but lack a complete executable price route.

The missing-stock problem

A missing exit quote can represent a merger, a delisting, a suspension or a data-routing problem. Selling the position for zero produces a scenario. It does not establish that the investor received nothing. Likewise, finding a merger headline does not establish when the investor could spend the proceeds.

The pilot’s newer period had already been inspected. Its results became development evidence, and later raw-volume corrections also superseded volume-dependent conclusions. This page deliberately avoids carrying those legacy return figures forward as current performance. Their value was diagnostic: they showed which accounting and validation problems needed to be solved.

The five-year sample also failed a simple design test. It contained 48 completed monthly return routes. The intended minimum was 60 training months, 12 test months and 36 final-holdout months—108 months before adding the effects of purge and embargo. The project could not honestly claim its intended validation design from that short window.

05 · 27–28 August

Acquire enough history, then protect the last test.

Maximum-history acquisition expanded the available date geometry to 345 observed month ends and 330 complete monthly routes. The first audit examined identities and date labels rather than newly available return values. That made it possible to agree on the study boundaries before seeing the outcomes those boundaries would select.

The approved design fixed twelve expanding folds, with at least five years of training and one year of testing. Purge and embargo rules separate information and holding intervals around the boundaries. A final 36-month historical holdout begins with the 1 June 2018 entry. It remains unread. The newer period beginning 30 June 2021 had already been inspected and was quarantined from claims of untouched evidence.

A statistical engine for joint block sign flips and a maximum-Sharpe comparison was implemented on invented data. The broader discipline follows the trial-aware evaluation discussed in Paleologo and the Quantopian lectures; the exact test protocol is a VHF engineering declaration. 06 · 6 September · Packages A–D

A fresh review found an economic error that tests had missed.

The September review changed the priority from adding research features to correcting and connecting the existing path. The central finding was a reversed share-volume conversion. Adjusted prices and adjusted volume move in opposite directions under a split. The old transform had applied the price direction to volume too.

Consider a raw price of $100 and a split-adjusted price of $50, with 2,000 split-adjusted shares traded. The corresponding raw volume is 1,000 shares: both descriptions represent $100,000 traded. The old code returned 4,000 raw shares, implying $400,000. This changes liquidity eligibility and can change the entire portfolio comparison. Existing tests had repeated the same error, so their success was insufficient.

07 · 7 September · Package E

Turn trial names into precise portfolios.

The registry had ten names, but several economic choices still needed definition before those names could produce holdings. Package E completed the rank-to-weight rules, short-side quality interpretation, constrained factor-neutral targets and the timing of quarterly, overlapping and seasonality-aware portfolios.

Overlapping portfolios require particular care. If three sleeves begin equally funded, their capital later drifts with their own outcomes. Rebalancing one sleeve must use that sleeve’s observed equity. It cannot quietly borrow an invented fresh allocation from the others. Neutrality is checked when a cohort is formed; older cohorts can subsequently drift away from those initial exposures.

The calendar audit checked 4,657 labels across 48 schedule/fold plans without opening provider price archives. It found that the first robustness fold had only 57–58 covered training months, below the required 60. That fold remained unavailable. All nine BIL-common folds passed the relevant geometry. The audit established usable schedules, not returns.

A target is a proposal, not a filled portfolio.

Package E can specify the portfolio the strategy wants. Replacing the current holdings still requires known account equity, tradable exits, admissible entries and sufficient cash. If full replacement fails, the system considers the declared exit policy; if that also fails, it keeps the actual holdings and records the block.

08 · 7 September · Package F

Discover what the proposed portfolios can actually do.

Package F connected construction to raw shares and the operational ledger. It added decision-time cost reserves, whole-share orders, opening risk and capacity checks, financing mechanics and separately funded cohorts. Raw shares matter because actual ownership, splits, distributions and sale proceeds must be accounted for in the units an investor holds.

The historical long-only adapter then ran five trials across nine folds and four execution scenarios: LOW, BASE and HIGH modeled costs plus a zero-rate reference. Each fold begins with $1 million. Each scenario carries its own capital and rounding outcomes. These are declared cost assumptions, not calibrated estimates of actual execution. The zero-rate reference retains whole-share and adverse tick-rounding rules; it is not a frictionless adjusted-price series.

The first run exposed a feasibility problem. Fifty 2% targets already exhaust the name-weight budget. Share quantities fixed at the preceding close can exceed a 2% opening limit after overnight price gaps. The full-batch rule therefore rejected many replacements. Only 30 of 1,275 scheduled net-scenario entries were accepted. A large number of available returns merely described cash sitting idle.

The next amendment declared a fixed, uncalibrated ±5% decision-price band, discounted the value of existing holdings for budgeting, and reserved costs on intended trade changes. It was specified before revised outcomes, without a parameter search. Actual opening prices could still veto trades; they could not be used retrospectively to choose better quantities. The second run accepted 203 entries.

The third amendment handled ordinary opening actions more accurately. Splits mechanically convert the already chosen share quantities. Dividend entitlement follows ownership before trading: an ex-date buyer does not inherit the seller’s carried-holder dividend. Whole-share exits can reduce exposure while retaining unresolved fractional claims. Modeled ex-date dividend payment remains an assumption, distinct from sourced actual payment timing.

09 · 6–7 September

A disappearing ticker leaves an economic claim.

The corrected monthly inventory identified 1,134 unique missing-exit events. Investigation was deliberately bounded to a frozen queue of twenty, prioritized by affected target allocations before reviewing their economics. The cap limits research effort; it does not remove the other events from the study.

Initial source review produced thirteen exact-cash term candidates, one fixed-cash floor candidate with an additional stock leg, and six unresolved term sets. None supplied the complete evidence needed for daily admission. Candidate consideration and usable cash are different things. Original source material, the conversion from source units to held shares, entitlement and actual cash or share-delivery timing must fit together.

Historical runner v4 connected a bounded reviewed-admission input to the existing claim ledger. Version 5 added an explicit review rule for a merger and its companion delisting, so one economic settlement does not leave a duplicate unresolved halt. Version 6 protects stock still owed when an intervening action affects the promised successor shares. Independent cash can arrive while an affected stock claim remains unresolved.

These later changes passed focused economic checks. The original admission remains zero out of twenty. The v3 outcomes above remain their own historical evidence. The separately declared ex-TECO calculation below now uses v6; it does not replace the original-universe results or supply the missing original settlement facts.

Why TECO became a case study—and why it is not indispensable

TECO Energy was a utility company acquired by Emera in 2016. Emera’s 1 July closing release confirms the acquisition and a $27.55-per-common-share cash right. It does not provide the complete admitted original-source and common-holder cash-availability evidence required by this daily accounting contract. A separate stub dividend also requires its own final amount, rounding and receipt facts.

An options-settlement document cannot establish when a passive common-stock holder received spendable cash. A listing-removal date is not a payment date. The missing facts therefore remain specific, rather than being replaced by a convenient assumption.

TECO is one event in the frozen queue, not the foundation of the investment thesis. Its evidence hunt has been deferred. Other development work and review of other queued events can continue. Removing it from selection is also possible as an explicitly separate, retrospective universe variation.

10 · 8–18 September · Position at that time

Reproduce the accounts, then establish the inputs.

The named ex-TECO development universe, implemented on 7 September, excludes the company by permanent identity before size thresholds, eligibility, ranking, shortlist selection and weight normalization. The rule is shared across all ten constructions and the equity comparators. BIL remains unchanged.

This timing is essential. Deleting a troublesome name after seeing its missing return would alter the analyzed outcome while hiding the selection change. Applying a documented policy before formation produces a different research variation with its own targets and identity. It still uses already inspected development data and does not recover an untouched test.

The implementation retains the full accounting data. An existing holding or legally received successor cannot vanish merely because the security is excluded from future selection; it still requires a valid valuation and exit. The 8 September session completed the actual monthly comparison, historical long-only execution, independent source reconstruction and a separate ownership audit. Those completed calculations were preserved when the exposure work resumed on 12 September. All calculations use the existing development inputs; the final holdout remains unread.

12 September: calculate and reproduce the signed seeds' risk geometry

The reviewed candidate was integrated without changing its calculation rules. Its 33 focused tests passed again in the working system, followed by the complete 604-test regression. Compilation, dependency checks, both worked examples and the frozen original example also passed. This established the integration checkpoint before the first actual exposure calculation.

The diagnostic uses the same declared ex-TECO development universe. For each formation it estimates beta from 252 daily returns, using an equal-weight market proxy built from the complete eligible universe. It standardizes log capitalization against that same universe and retains every selected sector constraint. It then asks whether the original signed seed can be adjusted while preserving its selected names, long and short signs, 50% on each side and 2% name caps, with market, sector and size exposures within the fixed numerical tolerance.

18 September: make borrowing, source checks and financing explicit

The next three implementation slices address different parts of signed execution. An opening borrow check can veto a fixed order when fresh evidence does not support the additional short. A shared lending ledger then reserves capacity across competing sleeves, consumes it on actual fills, releases it on evidenced events and carries loan recalls. A planned cover does not free capacity; an unsuccessful cover leaves the liability outstanding.

The source adapters bind observations to original records, revisions, clocks and account or loan scope. They rebuild exposures from the complete eligible universe and require continuous stock, cash and claim-fee coverage. Missing fees remain unknown; a recorded zero needs evidence. Financing is accrued in chronological order, including changes between trading events. These mechanics passed invented-example checks, reaching a 768-test implementation checkpoint. They have not run as a historically admitted signed strategy.

The local readiness review verified 230 consumed files and inspected four approved archive headers without decoding market-data rows. It found no admitted real package for any of the 11 source roles. This is a finding within the reviewed local inputs, not proof that suitable records do not exist elsewhere. The review tool's checkpoint passed 782 tests; the subsequent delivery-contract and documentation review changed no trading code and did not rerun the backtest.

Sharadar documents the candidate price and capitalization fields, and describes Daily metrics as point-in-time/as-reported. Its FAQ also identifies historical SIC classification changes. Those capabilities are useful. The review did not establish access to archived releases, per-version historical delivery proof or the required dated sector crosswalk. The next question is about those specific capabilities, rather than another broad market-data subscription. Stocks, Daily, FAQ.

IBKR's documented standard Flex history covers the current year and four previous calendar years: in 2026, that starts in 2022, after this study's 3 April 2017 development ceiling. Older saved reports or broker archives remain unknown. Its shortable search reports indicative availability and rates, which do not by themselves prove a reserved historical locate. The review checked public documentation, not anyone's private account or permissions. Flex history, shortable search.

There is also an accounting compatibility gap. IBKR describes tiered interest, settled balances and USD short collateral based on a rounded 102% price measure. VHF's present convention uses scalar rates and 100% marked-short collateral. Our inference is that an exact broker-account reconstruction needs a separate economic review and, where necessary, a model amendment; parsing a fee statement is insufficient. Current documentation does not certify pre-2017 terms. IBKR calculation rules.

A statement cannot document a trade that never happened

A historical account report can help reconcile that account's actual loans and charges. It does not establish capacity or terms for every alternative portfolio VHF simulates. A missing statement section might reflect filters, permissions or no actual position; it is not evidence of zero cost or unlimited borrowing.

11 · 20 September–7 October

Ask what different missing-payment assumptions would do.

On 20 September, contacting Sharadar was ruled out. The project changed direction: use the data already held and test explicit assumptions about payments that the historical records could not establish. This is an assumption-based study, called a “modeled study” in the engineering records. It asks what would happen if an assumption were true. It does not establish what investors actually received.

For stocks that stopped trading, four scenarios assumed either no recovery, half of a reference value after five trading days, or the full reference value after five or twenty trading days. The reference was the preceding reviewed closing price. The software kept an amount owed separate from cash received: a future payment could not finance today's purchase.

The program then combined five stock-selection and holding schedules, nine historical test periods, four trading-cost settings and four recovery assumptions. 5 × 9 × 4 × 4 = 720 simulated accounts. Each account starts with its own money. These are alternative experiments, not 720 independent market histories or parts of one fund.

The work encountered a small rounding error in a report total, which was corrected. A long run was paused on 20 September because of resource concerns. Later work resumed in stages. By 7 October, all 720 calculations and their planned repeat checks were recorded as complete. The last local continuation alone took about five hours and eighteen minutes. Earlier BASE-cost results retain their separate execution history; three original verification logs for that group are not available locally.

Finished computation did not mean complete investment evidence

Only 295 of the 720 accounts have a complete return path. The other 425 still encounter missing corporate-action terms, unresolved fractions of shares, or an inability to value and close the account at the end. Their whole-period returns remain unknown. A successful program can correctly report that the data cannot answer the question.

12 · 8–9 October

Repair two specific gaps, then learn from the saved results.

On 8 October, two limited changes were implemented and checked using separate result versions. The first corrected the order of final-sale and assumed-payment events at the end of a test period. All 32 selected accounts then had complete paths. This did not fill the other missing results in the original 720-account study.

The second change dealt with fractions left by a stock split. Under a declared assumption, a positive fraction becomes an amount owed at that event's opening price and is paid after trading at the next trading-day opening. This is a model of payment, not evidence of actual broker settlement. Of 48 selected accounts, 32 became complete; 16 remained incomplete. Neither change resolved unknown spinoff terms, where an investor receives shares in another company.

13 · 9–11 October

Extend the machinery to buying stocks and selling borrowed stocks.

“Signed” meant positive and negative portfolio weights. A positive weight represents shares owned. A negative weight represents shares sold short: borrowed, sold, and later bought back for return to the lender. It does not mean an approved or digitally signed result. From here, this guide calls these portfolios that buy and short stocks.

The historical evidence for stock borrowing and financing was still missing. The new work therefore declared eight hypothetical borrowing conditions: a reference case, zero financing rates, higher stock-borrow fees, higher interest on borrowed cash, a charge on short-sale collateral, half as many shares available at the opening, a ban on new shorts, and a request to return borrowed shares after a fixed holding interval. These are sensitivity tests, not observed historical broker terms.

The implementation joined those rules to orders, cash, share loans and repayments. It checked that two parts of the same portfolio cannot borrow the same available shares twice. A planned purchase does not return a loan until that purchase actually happens. Unsupported corporate actions and missing prices still leave results unknown.

On 10–11 October, work connected the saved portfolio-weight calculations to the existing development data. Reading and lookup were made more efficient. A narrow compatibility rule allowed the saved numerical calculations to be checked on the Mac without changing the original records or portfolio limits. Two independent preparations produced matching input tables.

The proposed first run retained 320 cases: five strategy versions × two test periods × four cost settings × eight borrowing assumptions. The periods are the first and last of the existing common development periods. This is a limited diagnostic, not the complete nine-period study. Every case could encounter missing inputs or unsupported actions; preparing its inputs did not guarantee a complete return.

14 · 13 October

The larger run exposed resource and bookkeeping problems.

The program first passed small invented examples and repeat calculations. Permission was then given for the specific 320-case run and a second full set of 320 calculations to check it. The real-data attempt stopped after about six minutes because memory pressure became too high. It produced no accepted completed case.

Store the growing trade record on disk.

The program had kept too much event history in memory. A repair wrote records incrementally to disk while preserving their contents. Small examples and independent repeats agreed. That showed the storage change worked on those examples; it did not yet prove a full run would finish.

Make unexpected stops explainable.

A later invented capacity run disappeared without a conclusive cause. Its cause remains unknown. An independent supervising process was added to record progress, exits and signals, and to stop the workers it owned. No successful result was inferred from the interrupted run.

Keep an untouched cash account available.

The next large invented test found that an account waiting in cash could be wrongly marked unavailable when another account started trading. The correction lets a genuinely idle cash account remain usable. It does not excuse a missing loan, payment, price or failed accounting calculation.

Then stop over a financially negligible rounding difference.

All six workers reached 1,200 positions. They then hit the same failure after many fee postings: cash and recorded cumulative costs differed by about 10−24 dollars, far below one cent. The check allowed only a slightly smaller difference. Investigation reproduced accumulated rounding as the cause. The run was stopped after about two hours; no memory guard had fired during that attempt.

A proposed fix would have changed how calculated charges were added to balances while retaining the extremely tight check. It was not implemented. Further repair was declined because the project had exceeded its time and resource budget. The system therefore remains stopped. The first real-data memory failure and the later invented rounding failure are different events.

What the latest software checks actually passed

After the idle-cash correction, 1,144 of 1,149 software tests passed. Four existing reference-output mismatches and one Linux-specific test error on the Mac remained. A separate set of 64 invented cases matched 64 fresh repeat calculations. These are software checks, not profitable portfolios. The later full-period capacity test still failed, so the whole system was not declared ready for another real-data run.

15 · Position reviewed 14 October

We have useful research evidence, but no validated strategy.

The work has delivered a research library, explicit portfolio rules, accounting software, saved assumption-based studies, and explanations of important failure points. It has also shown that missing payments and trading restrictions can dominate the result. That is a useful conclusion even though it does not identify a winning strategy.

The unfinished part is not just excessive decimal precision. The latest study of buying and shorting stocks has no accepted completed cases. Original historical borrowing evidence is still missing. The full comparison across all ten strategy versions and the reserved final test have not been completed. The tiny bookkeeping residual explains the latest stop; removing it would not solve those other problems.

On 13 October, a short finalization brief was prepared and published. It proposed clearer reporting, sensible accounting checks and a limited path to closure. It changed no calculation or study rule. This 14 October update expands the explanation and chronology; calculations remain stopped.

16 · Proposed next steps · Not yet approved for calculation

Finish the research at a cost its answer can justify.

My recommendation is to finish the current study as a documented research result using the evidence already saved. Its honest conclusion is that the tested assumptions and missing observations prevent a reliable strategy ranking. Do not make a profitable result, complete historical borrowing records, or a deployable trading system conditions for closing this project.

The final report should show, for each existing version, returns where known, worst peak-to-trough losses, costs, whether money was actually invested, and the number and reasons for missing results. It should compare like-for-like periods, explain the recovery sensitivity, and preserve all incomplete cases. Reading the saved results is sufficient for this endpoint; no new backtest is required.

Only if the remaining question about borrowing assumptions is worth more computation would I recommend one limited extension. Its output would explain how the eight hypothetical borrowing conditions affect trades and account values in the two selected periods. It would not prove historical tradability, establish a winner, or finish the full nine-period comparison.

Exactly what “change the acceptance requirements” means

This means changing the written rules used to accept a software calculation. It is not a legal contract or permission to change the investment idea. The proposal is to replace the near-zero accounting tolerance with a tightly bounded monetary check, and replace the second full 320-case calculation with a smaller independent check chosen before outcomes are seen. Both changes need to be written down and tested before use. The framework page explains each proposed change and its trade-off.
