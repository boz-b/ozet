Virtual Hedge Fund · from first principles

From an idea to an auditable portfolio.

VHF is not one clever formula. It is a chain of small decisions: what data was knowable, what the signal says, what risks hide inside it, how much to hold, whether a trade is allowed, how it fills, and whether the final record can be replayed.

The core implemented journey is daily US equities: decide after an eligible close, trade no earlier than the next regular open, and preserve enough evidence to reconstruct every result.

What might work?

The current central hypothesis is quality momentum: recent winners with steadier return paths may be better candidates than jump-driven winners.

Engineering

Can we calculate it honestly?

The code fixes timestamps, inputs, formulas, constraints, event order, and replay rules so later information cannot quietly improve an earlier decision.

Governance

What does the evidence permit?

A passing unit test permits a stronger code claim, not a return claim. Historical, holdout, capacity, paper, and live evidence are separate gates.

The two truths stay separate

The knowledge base records source-derived ideas. The engineering layer records which contracts, simulations, provider diagnostics, and experiments exist. A failed implementation does not make a research concept false; a green test does not make it profitable.

Chapter 02 · The information clock

A correct value at the wrong time is still wrong.

Most serious backtest errors are time errors. VHF therefore asks not only “what is the value?” but also “when did the system have permission to know it?”

Session T closesprices, universe, ADV and risk evidence become fixed
Decision is formedsignal → risk model → target → atomic approval
Next regular openthe first permitted execution point

Effective time

When the fact economically applies. A fiscal value may describe a quarter that ended months ago.

Availability time

When the value could actually be observed. This is the clock a decision must obey.

Revision

Which published version was visible then. A later correction must not rewrite an earlier experiment.

Point-in-time data means rebuilding the past as it looked then

VHF’s provider-neutral data contract separates permanent security identity, ticker history, exchange sessions, prices, actions, delistings, membership, market cap, liquidity, benchmarks, and factor exposures. Every requested session is materialized at its own cutoff.

Safe question

“Using only rows available by the June 24 close, what portfolio would the rules have formed?”

Unsafe shortcut

“Using today’s cleaned history, which stocks appear to have qualified on June 24?”

Identity is not the ticker

A company can change ticker; a ticker can later be reused. VHF keys history to a permanent security identity and treats symbols as dated labels. This protects holdings, corporate actions, and delistings from being attached to the wrong instrument.

The current Sharadar timing rule

A value economically dated to session T may influence VHF no earlier than the next reviewed regular session. The economic date is preserved; availability is never backdated to make same-close trading possible.

Step 3 — Keep the strongest decile, then judge path quality

Two stocks can both rise 30%. One may climb through many modest gains; the other may sit flat and jump on one spectacular day. VHF rewards the steadier path inside the high-momentum candidate set.

Information discreteness · FIP

Was the return broad or concentrated?

Across exactly 252 daily returns ending at the formation end, count positive and negative days. Zero-return days remain in the denominator.

ID = sign(momentum) × (negative fraction − positive fraction) For a winner: more negative ID = more continuous positive information.
MAX

Did one recent day dominate?

Take the largest signed daily return in the skipped month. A smaller MAX receives the better quality rank.

MAX = max(daily returns in skipped month) It is not max(abs(return)).
ID quality percentile = percentile rank of −ID MAX quality percentile = percentile rank of −MAX path quality = 50% × ID quality + 50% × MAX quality Candidate count = max(1, ceiling(eligible names × 10%)) The quality percentiles are calculated only among the frozen high-momentum candidates, not across the full universe.

Step 4 — Turn the rank into a provisional portfolio

Source-faithful long-only

Take the best path-quality names from the high-momentum decile, up to 50 names, equal weighted at 2% each. If fewer qualify, unused weight stays in cash.

Factor-controlled derivative

Use raw-momentum top and bottom deciles as long/short membership, 50% gross per side, 2% name cap, then apply a sign-preserving neutralization solve.

Daily challenger

A separate 252/21 prototype ranks 70% momentum, 20% log-path R² smoothness, and 10% low absolute jump. It is not allowed to replace the primary formula after results are seen.

Chapter 04 · Risk modeling

A return forecast is not a risk forecast.

Momentum ranks opportunity. A risk model estimates what can move together, which exposures dominate, and how violently a portfolio may behave.

Covariance is the grammar of joint movement

portfolio variance = weightsᵀ × covariance × weights portfolio volatility = √portfolio variance

Variance on the diagonal measures each stock’s own movement. Covariance off the diagonal measures how pairs move together. Those pairwise relationships are why a collection of individually volatile stocks can sometimes form a calmer portfolio—and why apparent diversification can disappear in a crisis.

Factor models split common risk from stock-specific risk

stock returns = factor exposures × factor returns + residual returns stock covariance = B × factor covariance × Bᵀ + diagonal(specific variance)

Exposure matrix B

How sensitive each stock is to market, sector, size, or another common driver.

Factor covariance

How those shared drivers vary and co-vary with one another.

Specific variance

The remaining stock-level uncertainty after the shared factors are removed.

The models are evaluated after the forecast

VHF scores candidates only on strictly later realized returns. It uses equal-weight, deterministic random dollar-neutral, and model-specific minimum-variance probes, then inspects calibration, QLIKE loss, eigenvalues, rank, conditioning, beta/loading stability, and model-induced turnover.

QLIKE(realized variance, forecast variance) = realized / forecast − log(realized / forecast) − 1 Zero is best. Large under- or over-forecasts are penalized, while the formula respects variance’s positive scale.

Alpha sizing keeps conviction, volatility and uncertainty separate

gross expected return = signal × return scale × confidence sizing score = gross expected return ÷ annualized volatility effective score = sizing score × (1 − uncertainty haircut)

A stronger signal may deserve more weight; a more volatile stock may deserve less; low confidence or high estimation uncertainty reduces the trade. None of these changes rewrites historical returns.

Chapter 05 · Portfolio construction

Keep the intended edge. Remove accidental bets.

Portfolio construction translates relative scores into weights while negotiating risk, liquidity, concentration, cost, and the positions already owned.

What “factor neutral” means

A raw momentum book may accidentally be long market beta, large technology sectors, or a particular size bucket. Neutralization changes weights as little as possible while bringing selected exposures to zero—or inside explicit bounds.

choose adjusted weights close to the desired weights subject to: net exposure ≈ 0 beta, sector and size exposure ≈ 0 gross exposure ≤ limit |single-name weight| ≤ cap predicted volatility ≤ limit turnover, cost and ADV participation ≤ limits
Phase 1

Least-change neutralizer

Start from ranked long and short books, then use a ridge-stabilized active-set solve for net, factor, gross, sign and name constraints. Volatility may scale down, never up.

Phase 5

One-dimensional convex path

Start from feasible holdings, form one covariance-scaled alpha direction, find the farthest structurally feasible endpoint, then choose how far to move along that line.

Pilot v7

Target-feasibility projection

Use a bounded least-change solve to preserve long/short signs, 50% gross per side and the 2% cap while neutralizing beta, size, net and every sector.

Why the one-dimensional path is useful

Cost and uncertainty can only shorten the move along a fixed direction. They cannot rotate the optimizer toward a completely different trade. Therefore, raising cost or uncertainty cannot increase turnover—a simple, testable safety property.

Actual v7 target diagnostics

The long-only target and raw long/short seed carry meaningful exposures. The controlled long/short target brings every registered exposure below 1e-10, but this proves target feasibility only; it does not provide realized factor-controlled returns.

Chapter 06 · Risk, execution and accounting

A target becomes real only through orders and a ledger.

The execution layer asks whether the whole rebalance is allowed, what adverse price it might receive, and whether cash, positions, actions, financing, and PnL reconcile afterward.

The pre-trade decision is atomic

VHF approves or rejects the complete batch. A rejected rebalance exposes no stray order that another component could execute.

Universal blockers

Invalid or stale data, unknown instruments, session mismatch, missing borrow, non-positive equity, duplicate lifecycle events, or a halted portfolio.

Portfolio limits

Gross, net, single name, turnover, liquidity participation, factor exposure, and drawdown.

Governance warnings

Signal decay, strategy drawdown, unstable parameters, or expected anomaly pain requiring review.

Turnover measures how much of the risky book changes

one-way turnover = ½ × Σ |target weight − current weight|

The half prevents counting a matched sell-and-buy rebalance twice. In the five-year pilot, mean target turnover is 61.67%; return drift moves it to roughly 62.5–62.9%, depending on the terminal-cash sensitivity.

The default synthetic fill is deliberately adverse

participation = |order notional| ÷ ADV impact fraction = 0.10 × daily volatility × √participation buy fill = next raw open × (1 + half spread + impact) sell fill = next raw open × (1 − half spread − impact) commission = 1 bp × |reference notional|

The model uses prior-close ADV and volatility at the next open; it never peeks at that day’s later volume or close. Phase 4 then adds whole lots, adverse ticks, partial fills, cancel/expiry, and point-in-time borrow.

Capacity finds the first liquidity bottleneck

participationᵢ(AUM) = AUM × |Δweightᵢ| ÷ ADVᵢ capacity = largest constant AUM for which every leg ≤ 5% of ADV

The pilot evaluates every nonzero buy and sell. One missing route, ADV value, or volatility estimate makes the entire capacity path unavailable. The current fixed-slice sensitivity finds a history-wide bottleneck near $2.4 million; it is arithmetic under registered assumptions, not calibrated executable capacity.

The ledger is the final arithmetic authority

holdings value = Σ(shares × raw close) equity = cash + holdings value daily PnL = equity[t] − equity[t−1] starting capital + cumulative daily PnL = ending equity

Phase 4 separately tracks realized and unrealized gross PnL, spread, impact, commission, tick rounding, dividends, splits, known delisting proceeds, and exact short-financing intervals. Missing terminal treatment preserves the unresolved holding, halts trading in it, and blocks finalization instead of inventing zero recovery.

A halt is persistent

A drawdown or operational breach can stop new risk and schedule liquidation. The system never silently restarts itself; only a separately identified, audited human reset can clear the halt.

Walk-forward evaluation preserves chronology

expanding training: at least 5 years test window: 1 year purge: 21 sessions embargo: 21 sessions final holdout: 3 years, locked before inspection

The purge removes training observations whose returns overlap the test boundary. The embargo leaves a buffer after the test. With maximum history now available, this geometry has been materialized as 12 expanding folds and a separately locked historical holdout. The holdout has not been opened.

Multiple testing asks whether the best result could be luck

VHF preregisters a block sign-flip maximum-Sharpe procedure across the complete ten-trial momentum family. Whole 21-session blocks receive one shared random sign across every trial, preserving local dependence and the competition among strategies. Ten thousand repetitions estimate how often noise produces a family maximum at least as strong as the observed one.

p = (1 + number of simulated family maxima ≥ observed maximum) ÷ (1 + 10,000)

Chapter 08 · The longer-history experiment

More history changed what could be tested—not what could be claimed.

The original five-year slice contained too little time to separate learning, testing, and a final untouched examination. Extending Sharadar to its maximum history crossed that mathematical threshold and allowed the approved Phase 6 experiment to be built honestly.

Why 108 months was the hard minimum

The frozen design needs at least five years to learn, one year to test, and three final years to hold back. That is 60 + 12 + 36 = 108 monthly outcomes before allowing for the 21-session safety gaps. The first slice supplied only 48. The maximum-history calendar supplies 330 completed monthly routes.

Do not read the raw-momentum loss as a verdict on momentum

Chapter 09 · The wider research laboratory

The core engine now hosts several controlled research families.

These Phase 8 modules freeze definitions and timing before real testing. They are prerequisites and diagnostics—not competing performance claims.

Lead–lag statistical arbitrage

Did one instrument react before another?

Fit a cutoff-safe ordinary least-squares relationship from lead evidence known at the decision close to a target return beginning at the next open. H1 and H3 horizons must agree on shared evidence.

target return ≈ intercept + beta × lead feature

Adjacent reviews detect sign flips, beta drift and correlation decay. Hash-bound state then derives ENTRY, HOLD, EXIT or NO_TRADE.

Technical overlay

A dimmer switch, never a new signal

Exactly 80 total-return closes supply close versus SMA40, 40-observation momentum, and 20-return volatility versus a warmed historical median.

overlay magnitude ∈ [0, base magnitude] direction cannot reverse

The overlay may suppress or attenuate a complete base signal. It cannot create, reverse, or amplify alpha.

Value and value–momentum

Cheap fundamentals meet strong price action

The value characteristic uses latest visible total-issuer common equity over trailing 30-calendar-day average total-issuer market cap.

book-to-price = common equity ÷ average market cap composite = 50% value percentile + 50% momentum percentile

The synthetic boundary emits ranks only. It does not silently intersect mismatched universes or allocate capital.

Alternative data and ML

First prove the feature’s identity

A seven-UTC-day entity-activity count resolves visible revisions and dated entity-to-security mappings. Missing mapping is not the same as a genuine observed zero.

The ML layer declares a next-open-to-close direction target, L2 logistic primary, shallow boosted-tree challenger, and time-aware protocol. It contains no labels, fitted models, predictions, metrics, or winner yet.

Variance and correlation diagnostics

Bennett’s variance-first view appears in two fixed synthetic calculations. Variance is used because squared moves add naturally and option/correlation mathematics is expressed in variance units.

forward variance(T₁,T₂) = (variance₂ × T₂ − variance₁ × T₁) ÷ (T₂ − T₁)
index variance = Σ wᵢ²σᵢ² + 2ρ Σᵢ<ⱼ wᵢwⱼσᵢσⱼ

VHF binds exact 30/90-day ACT/365F maturities and a fixed three-name constant-correlation example. An implied correlation outside the mathematically valid interval is rejected, not clipped into looking plausible.

Attribution explains what happened without double counting

market movement = market factor + nonmarket factor + idiosyncratic PnL total PnL = market movement + hedge + corporate action − spread − impact − commission − tick rounding − borrow + residual idiosyncratic IR = selection IR × diversification ratio + sizing IR

The first two lines are currency. The last line is a unitless research-quality decomposition and stays separate. Phase 7 also tracks information coefficient, hit rate, signal drawdown, parameter drift, capacity multiple, and execution dislocation.

Momentum + factor control

Chapter 12 · Compact glossary

The terms worth keeping.

A small vocabulary carries most of the project.

Alpha
A forecast of relative return not yet converted into a position.
ADV
Average daily dollar volume; a liquidity scale used for participation and capacity.
Basis point
One hundredth of one percent. One bp = 0.01%.
Covariance
How two returns vary together, in squared-return units.
Drawdown
The decline from the portfolio’s previous high-water mark.
Exposure
A portfolio’s sensitivity to a shared driver such as market beta, sector or size.
Factor
A common return driver shared by many securities.
Gross exposure
The sum of absolute portfolio weights: long plus short risk.
Net exposure
The sum of signed weights: long exposure minus short exposure.
Half-life
The time after which an EWMA observation carries half its original weight.
Holdout
A final untouched sample opened only after research choices are frozen.
Idiosyncratic
The stock-specific part left after common factor movement is removed.
Market impact
Adverse price movement associated with the size of the fund’s own trade.
Momentum
Relative strength over a specified past horizon.
Point in time
Reconstructing only the facts and revisions available at that historical decision.
Positive semidefinite
A covariance property ensuring no portfolio is assigned impossible negative variance.
Rebalance
Changing current holdings toward a new target portfolio.
Replay
Rebuilding recorded accounting from events without recomputing the strategy.
Residual
The unexplained remainder after an accounting or statistical decomposition.
Shrinkage
Pulling a noisy estimate toward a simpler, more stable target.
Spread
The gap between buy and sell prices; half-spread is one side’s modeled cost.
Survivorship bias
Studying only securities that survived to the present and silently losing failed ones.
Turnover
The fraction of the book replaced during a rebalance.
Volatility

Ten questions for any VHF result

  1. What exact universe existed at each decision?
  2. When was every input actually available?
  3. Did the signal use total-return data while execution used raw prices?
  4. Which formula was primary, and which variants were registered challengers?
  5. What factor, volatility, concentration and liquidity risks remain?
  6. How much trading was required, and under what cost assumptions?
  7. How were missing entries, delistings and terminal proceeds treated?
  8. Were folds chronological, purged and embargoed, with a locked holdout?
  9. How many strategies were tried, and where is the multiple-testing adjustment?
  10. What evidence stage does the result actually permit?
