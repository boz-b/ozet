01 · Concepts

What could this trade do
to the account’s risk?

Project state · 13 October 2026 · Europe/Istanbul

Intraday-risk assesses a proposed trade against the whole account: possible near-term losses, the cost of closing positions, stressed exposures and the reliability of the evidence. It is a risk check, not a signal that finds profitable trades.

Trade intent
A proposed instrument, side, quantity and order terms. It has not become a confirmed fill.
Exposure
Shares × price. A long position gains when the price rises; a short position loses. Gross exposure adds absolute values; net exposure allows offsets.
Forecast horizon
The period being assessed: 5, 15 or 30 minutes. This differs from the time the computer takes to return an answer.
VaR · loss quantile
A loss threshold at a chosen model confidence level. A 99% VaR describes a modeled tail, not a maximum possible loss.
Stress scenario
A specified adverse market move or trading condition. It asks “what if?”, without claiming that the event has a measured probability.
Durable result
The decision and required evidence are safely recorded before a usable answer returns. A number briefly held in memory is not enough.

Current boundary The software calculates and records diagnostics. It does not authorize or send orders.

02 · Required inputs

A complete picture of the account.

The tool needs both values and their context: which instrument they describe, when they were observed, when they became available, and which version is current. Fast calculations on stale or incomplete inputs are still unusable.

Unfilled orders are a range of possible holdings.

With 100 shares, an outstanding buy of 50 and sell of 80, the possible holding is 20–150 shares. It is not necessarily 70: either order can fill first, partly, or not at all. Pending cancellations remain included until cancellation is confirmed.

03 · The five risk checks

Different questions need different measures.

The project calls its components F (forecast), L (liquidity), P (portfolio stress), V (validation) and G (gate). Their limits remain separate. Adding VaR, stress loss and exit cost into one total would count some price moves more than once.

F

Forecast losses over 5, 15 and 30 minutes

Method: a time-of-day-adjusted, multivariate EWMA model, followed by Monte Carlo simulation. EWMA is an exponentially weighted moving average: recent observations receive more weight. The model’s covariance describes how instruments vary and move together; simulation turns that estimate into possible future price paths.

  1. Remove the usual time-of-day effect. Scale completed one-minute returns by a pattern learned from earlier data, so a normally busy opening minute is not confused with an unexpected shock.
  2. Update joint variability. Combine the previous covariance estimate with the newest return movement. Stabilize noisy estimates using declared rules; reject invalid estimates.
  3. Reuse historical joint shocks. Standardize and resample whole historical vectors, keeping the instruments’ joint shock structure rather than drawing each stock independently.
  4. Simulate forward. Generate many 30-minute paths. Each path updates its covariance minute by minute. Restore the time-of-day pattern for the actual request time.
  5. Measure portfolio losses. Apply fixed holdings to each simulated path and select the declared loss percentile at each horizon. Also report covariance for other checks.
Assumptions and the key calculation

For each simulated outcome, loss = −Σ(shares × starting price × simulated return). Sorting these losses gives the empirical VaR. The raw quantile is retained; the operational loss figure is floored at zero.

The model uses zero conditional log-return drift and assumes standardized shock vectors can be sampled independently over future minutes. Neither assumption makes the model correct by construction. Training, parameter tuning and final validation use separate chronological periods. Model adequacy, tail precision and residual dependence need real-data evidence.

Previously simulated shocks may be reused only while their state and settings remain valid. A later intent needs its own time-of-day projection and current marks. Reuse cannot refresh an old input’s age.

L

Estimate what it would cost to close the position

Method: walk the visible order book. Sell a long position through bids, or buy back a short through asks, using each level’s available quantity. Compare the weighted execution price with the current midpoint and include the required fees.

Repeat with adverse prices, wider spreads and less depth. Report price movement, trading friction and total liquidation loss separately. If depth does not cover the whole position, the full cost is unknown; the tool does not invent liquidity beyond the last visible quote.

A small book-walking example

Illustrative only: selling 100 shares against 60 shares at $99.90 and 40 at $99.80 produces an average price of $99.86. Against a $100 midpoint, the price cost is $14, plus fees. With only 80 shares of visible bids, the cost of selling all 100 cannot be established.

This is an immediate-exit estimate from a snapshot. It does not predict replenishment, future fills or cross-asset market impact. Missing required fees also prevent a verified all-in cost.

P

Test volatility, broken hedges and explicit shocks

Method: compare ordinary portfolio volatility with higher volatility and a hedge-failure scenario in which held positions can lose together. Then apply approved market, sector and individual-name shocks. Check gross/net exposure, leverage and concentration as well.

Each required scenario must pass its own limit. The worst covariance scenario is used instead of averaging favorable and unfavorable cases. For unfilled orders, conservative bounds cover possible holdings; a hedge that works only after both legs fill is insufficient.

Three distinctions that matter

Dollar volatility is a standard deviation, not VaR or a worst possible loss. Shock loss is the loss under one specified move, not a probability forecast. A fill-range bound can overstate the risk of any one attainable portfolio, but protects against assuming a convenient fill order.

Historical daily shocks stay daily/event scenarios. They are not rescaled and presented as calibrated five-minute probabilities. Scenario coverage cannot omit an instrument merely because its history is missing.

V

Check whether the model’s forecasts deserve trust

Method: save the forecast, then compare it with the later price outcome for the holdings frozen at the forecast time. Later strategy trades must not change the result being scored.

The Kupiec test checks whether losses exceed VaR too often. The Christoffersen test checks whether those exceptions cluster. Separate checks compare predicted costs with actual fills and fees. A small sample or missing endpoints yields insufficient evidence, not an automatic pass.

How the validation avoids an easy but misleading pass

Declare the sample schedule before looking at results. Use eligible, non-overlapping windows for primary tests, preserve missed forecasts, and do not join exception sequences across missing data or session changes. Non-overlap alone does not prove independence.

Corrections append to the record rather than erase it. Live fills, paper fills and observable-book proxies remain distinct evidence. Validation may recommend degrading a model; it cannot increase a limit or clear a stop by itself.

G

Combine the evidence into one decision

Method: check identity, account state, freshness, order terms, cash, limits, model health and stop conditions; then require the applicable F/L/P/V results. Every result must refer to the same current context.

A failed, stale, missing or unqualified required check blocks new risk. The same intent cannot be reused with different contents. A controlled-reduction calculation exists for narrowly defined cases, but reduction is disabled. It must prove the required risk measures do not worsen during partial completion; a smaller final position alone is insufficient.

04 · One trade assessment

From a supplied intent to a recorded answer.

This is the first use case selected for the next requirements record. The request supplies the trade; Part 1 assesses it. It does not choose the portfolio or make the trade smaller until it passes.

  1. 01
    Receive & admit

    Validate the request and record accepted queue state. Include waiting time in the response time.

  2. 02
    Freeze a coherent context

    Bind the current account revision, market inputs, model, policy, clock and expiry. Form before/after fill ranges.

  3. 03
    Run the required checks

    Use valid prepared forecasts where permitted; project for this origin and run liquidity, stress and health checks. Missing preparation is part of the wait or causes refusal.

  4. 04
    Recheck & save

    Confirm that sources, revisions and expiry still hold. Write the required decision and evidence before returning.

  5. 05
    Return the diagnostic

    Report what the gate would decide, reasons and evidence references. Today this creates no executable approval or broker reservation.

Complete response timeadmission + queue wait + required preparation + checks + durable return

Expiry, lost essential events, corrupt records or failed writes leave the system unable to approve new risk. Restart must verify saved history before becoming ready. These checks explain why measuring only the numerical kernel misses much of the actual response.

Solver and safety boundary

The numerical backend uses Clarabel with scaled inputs, one bounded solve at a time and explicit time/memory controls. Financial checks are reconstructed after solving; a numerical tolerance cannot waive an original cash or risk limit.

Pure candidate calculations reuse explicitly bound immutable inputs. They should not refit the forecast or rebuild and write the entire evidence store for every candidate. The outer request still verifies current sources and saves the final evidence. Real return, volume, fee and covariance profiles remain separate inputs requiring admission.

07 · What the evidence says

Correct answers still need a useful response time.

The original engineering suggestions were under 10 seconds for scheduled forecast work and under 500 milliseconds for a cached assessment. They were not established service guarantees. The integrated path missed them.

Earlier, the 23 September instrumented release run recorded 40.077 seconds for an origin and 92.118 seconds for an assessment. Those results used a different source and measurement setup; they are not a like-for-like speedup comparison with October.

The architecture review ended without an accepted solution.

Two offline proposals were assessed: reuse decoding within one request, and prepare an immutable generation before requests arrive. Neither established both equivalent safety/integrity checks and a credible complete-response budget. The assessment ended NO_GO after two of two attempts. That does not prove 500 ms impossible; it means neither proposal justified an implementation experiment.

A separate Linux solver refusal also remains unexplained. A later passing check does not explain the original failure. Existing diagnostic and codec changes are preserved as unfinished work, not promoted into the delivered software.

08 · What remains

Define useful operation, then qualify it.

Development is paused. The selected future response is a complete durable Part 1 assessment of a supplied intent. Actual arrival patterns, useful deadlines, freshness limits, workload and host resources have not yet been decided through use of the intended setup.

Requirements

Define the operating contract

Decide who supplies intents, how frequently they arrive, the largest account covered, allowable input ages, and useful success/refusal deadlines. The B3 draft (#188) records the use-case choice; the remaining values are still open.

Evidence

Resolve failure and timing blockers

Give the retained solver failure an evidence-backed disposition (#177). For latency (#178), establish stage costs and any safe remedy against the chosen requirement. Further design work needs a new bounded scope and new evidence or requirements; the exhausted review is not a retry queue.

Qualification

Test broader cases, then the actual host

After the preceding gates or an explicit exception, test the declared 1/2/4/8-name workload coverage, queues, failures and recovery. Then measure the frozen runtime on the actual Trading Pi. Both stages of #179 are unstarted; eight names is a current capability ceiling, not a qualified service size.

Real operation

Admit sources, models, policy and account authority

Obtain suitable data/depth/fees, calibrate and independently validate the model, approve limits, and establish complete authoritative account/reservation state. Finish host integration, controlled observation and operational delivery. These are independent requirements; fast synthetic calculations cannot replace them.
